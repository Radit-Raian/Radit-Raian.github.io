Lumin Diaries

Through the Eyepiece

Frames from the Edge of Infinity

"Every photon here has traveled longer than memory."

These are not photographs — they are time machines. Light captured from stellar nurseries, dark towers of dust, and blooms of ionized hydrogen that predate every civilization on Earth. Each frame in Lumin Diaries is a quiet reminder that the universe has been writing poetry long before we learned to read it.

Some of these nebulae will dissolve in five million years. Others are still mid-breath, collapsing into stars. All of them were there before us — and all of them will outlast us.

September 20–27, 2025

Days with Sirius at 43.6° North

For the first time, I carried Bangladesh beyond its borders in a quiet responsibility that weighed and inspired all at once at the Open World Astronomy Olympiad 2025. Home was 5500 kilometers away, yet every calculation felt tethered to it. At 43.6° north, the nights were astonishingly clear and unfamiliar. Sirius burned brighter than ever — not merely a point of light, but a companion through endless hours of thought, doubt, and discovery. "The sky was not the limit there."
